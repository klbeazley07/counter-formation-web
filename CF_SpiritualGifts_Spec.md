## 1. CONTEXT: WHAT THIS TOOL IS

Counter Formation is a Christian spiritual formation brand built on Ephesians 6:10-18. The mission: equip Christians with the theology, practices, and daily rhythms needed to live intentionally in a world designed for drift. The tagline is *Formed in Christ. Living Counter to Culture.*

The Field Guide is Counter Formation's library of formation tools. The first anchor of the Field Guide is the Fruit of the Spirit Assessment -- a diagnostic that surfaces where the Spirit is forming character in the user right now. The Spiritual Gifts Assessment is the second anchor.

Both tools share a single theological premise: the Spirit's work in a believer is real, active, and visible. The Fruit assessment asks who I am becoming. The Gifts assessment asks how God has wired me to contribute. Both are formational, not diagnostic in the personality-test sense. Both are designed to send the user back into the body of Christ -- not into self-knowledge for its own sake.

This component is more ambitious than the Fruit assessment in three ways: it adds a community-confirmation flow that integrates trusted-person responses into the user's results, it includes a dedicated exploration experience (the Gift Constellation) for users who want to learn about the gifts without committing to the full assessment, and it operates on a continuationist theological foundation that requires careful pastoral framing for the more contested gifts.

## 2. THEOLOGICAL FRAMING -- NON-NEGOTIABLE

This section is the foundation of the entire instrument. Read it before writing a single line of copy or designing a single screen. The framing here determines whether the assessment is a serious formation tool or another personality quiz with Bible verses attached.

### 2.1 What a Spiritual Gift Actually Is

Counter Formation's Spiritual Gifts Assessment is built on the continuationist framework articulated most clearly by Sam Storms. A spiritual gift is not a static trait the believer possesses. It is the Holy Spirit manifesting his presence and imparting his power through individual believers to enable them to exceed the limitations of their humanity so that they might faithfully fulfill specific ministry tasks for the building up of the body of Christ.

This definition does important work. A gift is not something Claude or anyone else can give you a label for. It is God himself, present and active through you, expressed in specific moments for specific purposes. The instrument must never frame gifts as personality categories or fixed identities. It frames them as evidence of where the Spirit is currently working through the user.

### 2.2 The Continuationist Position

This assessment takes a continuationist position: all of the gifts described in the New Testament are active in the church today. The Holy Spirit continues to gift believers across the full range of manifestations described by Paul -- including the more contested gifts of prophecy, healing, miracles, discernment of spirits, and tongues with interpretation.

This position is held with pastoral restraint. Continuationism does not mean every strong impression is from the Spirit, every claim of prophecy is reliable, or every charismatic experience is unambiguously God. New Testament prophecy itself is fallible and must be tested. Discernment of spirits requires accountability. Healing is real and rare, not formulaic. The instrument treats these gifts with the same theological seriousness as the ministry and equipping gifts, and with the same insistence that they be exercised in community, under scripture, with the fruit of the Spirit as the diagnostic of authentic operation.

### 2.3 Gifts Are Confirmed in the Body

Paul's logic in 1 Corinthians 12 is that gifts are given for the common good and recognized in the body. The modern inventory tradition has subtly inverted this -- you discover your gift through self-report, then go find a place to use it. The New Testament order is the opposite: serve the body, and the Spirit confirms the gift through fruitful service and the witness of those around you.

This assessment puts the New Testament order back in place. Self-report alone is methodologically and theologically insufficient. A user might score high on inclination toward a gift but have no record of fruitfulness in that area, and the people closest to them might never have seen the gift function. That gap is important diagnostic information, not a verdict, but an invitation to test the gift in actual service.

### 2.4 Gifts and Fruit Are Inseparable

Galatians 5:22-23 and 1 Corinthians 12 describe two dimensions of the Spirit's work in the same believer. The fruit shows who you are becoming. The gifts show how the Spirit moves through you to build up others. A gift exercised without the fruit becomes noise -- 1 Corinthians 13 is Paul's explicit warning about this. The fruit cultivated in isolation from the body never reaches the people it was meant to bless.

The Field Guide treats these as a unified system. After a user completes both assessments, the results screen surfaces the integration: your formation fruit (where the Spirit is shaping your character) and your active gifts (how the Spirit is moving through you) together form a single picture of how God is at work in your life right now.

### 2.5 Gifts Function in Clusters, Not in Isolation

The body of Christ in 1 Corinthians 12 is not a collection of individuals exercising independent gifts. It is a single body where each gift functions in relationship to others. Teaching functions best alongside Shepherding. Mercy functions alongside Exhortation. Leadership requires Administration to be sustainable. The instrument honors this throughout -- the body-application section of each gift identifies the gifts it typically pairs with, and the Gift Constellation visualizes those relationships as connecting lines in the interface.

This is not decorative. It is a theological commitment built into the architecture: no believer is meant to function alone, and no gift is meant to operate in isolation.

### 2.6 Gifts and Seasons

The New Testament does not give a tidy systematic answer to whether spiritual gifts are given once and permanently or given according to the Spirit's sovereign distribution in particular seasons. The strongest continuationist framework, articulated by Sam Storms and Wayne Grudem and consistent with Paul's emphasis on the Spirit's sovereign will in 1 Corinthians 12:11, holds that both are true.

Some gifts function as sustained, identity-shaping giftings that align with calling and personality over the course of a life. The ministry gifts of Romans 12 (teaching, exhortation, serving, giving, leadership, mercy) and the equipping gifts of Ephesians 4 (evangelism, shepherding) often operate this way -- they tend to be the gifts a believer carries across decades, the consistent shape of how the Spirit has wired them to contribute to the body.

Other gifts function more situationally. The manifestation gifts of 1 Corinthians 12 (wisdom, knowledge, faith, healing, miracles, prophecy, discernment) often operate this way in the New Testament narratives -- the Spirit becomes visible through a believer in specific moments, specific seasons, or specific ministry assignments. Peter speaks a word of knowledge to Ananias once. Agabus prophesies a famine. A believer who has functioned in healing during one season of ministry may not in another. This is not failure or loss; it is the Spirit's sovereignty.

This assessment honors both dimensions. The results are framed as "where the Spirit is at work in you right now" rather than as a permanent giftedness profile. The Active / Emerging / Quiet tiers reflect what is visible in this season. The instrument is designed to be retaken as seasons change. A gift that is Quiet in one season may become Active in another. A gift that is Active now may shift as ministry context, life circumstances, or the Spirit's distribution changes. The picture is real, and it is also temporal. The Spirit gives his gifts as he wills, for the building up of the body in the moment.

A practical implication: a believer should not freeze their identity around the results of a single assessment. The picture is meant to send them into service, not into self-classification. As they serve faithfully in the gifts that are active now, the Spirit may surface new gifts, deepen existing ones, or shift the pattern as the needs of the body and the calling of God develop over time.

## 3. METHODOLOGY: THREE STREAMS, ONE PICTURE

This is the methodological core of the instrument and what distinguishes it from every other spiritual gifts tool currently in the market. Most existing assessments are pure self-report on a single dimension: do you think you have the gift. That methodology measures self-perception, not the Spirit's actual work. This instrument measures three streams separately and integrates them.

### Stream 1 -- Inclination

Self-report on the pattern of where the user senses the Spirit working through them. Behavioral and situational questions, not aspirational. Three questions per gift, five-point agreement scale. This is what every existing instrument measures.

### Stream 2 -- Fruitfulness

Self-report on outcomes the user has actually observed when they have exercised the gift. The fruitfulness question asks for evidence of the Spirit's work, not capacity. Did the person change? Did the body get built up? Did good come of it? This stream catches the user who scores high on inclination but has never actually seen the Spirit move through them in that area. One question per gift, five-point frequency scale.

### Stream 3 -- Community Confirmation

Built into the instrument itself, not bolted on as a suggestion afterward. After the user completes streams 1 and 2, they send a shareable invitation to two or three trusted people in their life. Those people complete a short, third-person version of the assessment about the user -- one question per gift, asking whether they have observed the gift functioning. Their responses integrate into the user's final score.

This stream is what makes the instrument theologically honest. The New Testament logic is that gifts are confirmed in community. The methodology has to operationalize that, not just suggest it.

### Composite Scoring

Each gift receives three sub-scores, normalized 0-100: inclination, fruitfulness, and confirmation (averaged across trusted persons who respond). The composite is weighted:

- **Inclination: 30 percent**
- **Fruitfulness: 30 percent**
- **Confirmation: 40 percent**

Confirmation carries the heaviest weight because the body confirming the Spirit's work is the New Testament test.

Results are displayed in three tiers based on composite score and confirmation status:

- **Active gifts** (composite ≥ 70 with confirmation present): full formation output, body-application, anchor scripture, weekly practice.
- **Emerging gifts** (composite 50-69, or 70+ without confirmation): honest framing -- this may be a gift the Spirit is developing in you, or an inclination that hasn't yet borne visible fruit. Test it in service.
- **Quiet areas** (composite below 50): not weaknesses. Gifts the Spirit hasn't visibly emphasized in this season. The output notes that some gifts are also commands -- hospitality, mercy, serving, giving, evangelism -- and apply to all believers regardless of giftedness.

### Charismatic Gifts -- Handled Separately

Tongues and interpretation of tongues are assessed with a different question format that honors their distinct manner of manifestation. Each charismatic gift receives a direct-experience question and a fruitfulness question (two questions total per gift, rather than four). The trusted-person side does not include questions on these gifts, since their manifestation is rarely public in the way teaching, mercy, or leadership is. Framing copy in the assessment explains this approach with pastoral honesty: these gifts are real and active in the church today, but they manifest in ways the standard methodology cannot reliably surface.

## 4. THE GIFT LIST -- 17 CORE GIFTS PLUS 2 CHARISMATIC GIFTS

The instrument assesses 17 core gifts using the standard three-stream methodology. Two additional charismatic gifts (tongues and interpretation of tongues) are assessed with the modified format described above. Apostleship is folded into Leadership -- the pioneering function is addressed in the formation output for high-Leadership scorers whose responses indicate it.

### Manifestation Gifts (1 Corinthians 12:7-11)
1. **Wisdom** -- the Spirit-given utterance of God's wisdom into a specific situation
2. **Knowledge** -- the Spirit-given utterance of knowledge that natural means could not produce
3. **Faith** -- the special measure of confidence to trust God against circumstances
4. **Healing** -- the Spirit's power working through the believer to bring physical or spiritual healing
5. **Miracles** -- the Spirit's power producing acts that exceed the ordinary course of nature
6. **Prophecy** -- the Spirit-given word for a person, group, or situation, given for upbuilding, encouragement, and consolation
7. **Discernment of Spirits** -- the Spirit-given ability to distinguish what is of God, the flesh, or the enemy

### Ministry Gifts (Romans 12, 1 Peter 4)
8. **Teaching** -- the Spirit-given capacity to communicate scripture and theology in a way that builds understanding and produces spiritual growth
9. **Exhortation** -- the Spirit-given ability to come alongside others to encourage, strengthen, and call them forward in faith
10. **Serving (Helps)** -- the Spirit-given inclination and ability to meet practical needs that enable other gifts to function
11. **Giving** -- the Spirit-given capacity to fund the Kingdom with extraordinary generosity, cheerfulness, and discernment
12. **Leadership** -- the Spirit-given capacity to direct, mobilize, and care for others toward Kingdom purpose
13. **Mercy** -- the Spirit-given capacity to enter into the suffering of others and produce comfort, healing, or presence that brings them to God
14. **Hospitality** -- the Spirit-given capacity to welcome people into one's life or home in a way that creates spaces of belonging
15. **Administration** -- the Spirit-given capacity to organize, plan, and steward resources and people toward the body's mission

### Equipping Gifts (Ephesians 4:11)
16. **Evangelism** -- the Spirit-given capacity to proclaim the gospel in ways that draw people to genuine faith in Christ
17. **Shepherding (Pastoring)** -- the Spirit-given capacity to take long-term responsibility for the spiritual care, feeding, and protection of a flock

### Charismatic Gifts (handled separately, 2 questions each)
- **Tongues (Various Kinds of Tongues)** -- the Spirit-given ability to speak in a language not learned, for prayer, worship, or proclamation
- **Interpretation of Tongues** -- the Spirit-given ability to make a tongue intelligible to the gathered body

## 5. THE GIFT CONSTELLATION -- EXPLORATION EXPERIENCE

This is the gift exploration experience accessed from the assessment intro screen. The strategic frame: the Constellation is the on-ramp to the assessment, not a competing destination. Users who feel intimidated by jumping into a 12-15 minute assessment can explore the gifts first, then return to the assessment with deeper understanding.

### 5.1 Purpose and Strategic Frame

The Constellation answers a real user need that no other Christian formation tool currently addresses well: people lack awareness of what the gifts actually are. Most gift assessments assume users already know what they are walking into. They don't. A user who has heard of "spiritual gifts" but has never been taught what they mean is being asked to make accurate self-assessments about something they cannot define. The Constellation closes that gap.

It also serves as a discoverable destination in the Field Guide for users who don't intend to take the assessment yet. They can encounter the gifts contemplatively, learn what the Spirit might be doing through them and others, and decide later whether they're ready for the diagnostic.

### 5.2 Visual Concept

The 19 gifts are rendered as points of light against the Hero Black background. Not cards. Not tiles. Luminous marks suggesting stars in a constellation, each with the gift name beneath it in Michroma small caps, Champagne Gold. The arrangement is intentional and theological: Manifestation gifts at the top (the seven gifts that flow directly from the Spirit), Ministry gifts in the middle (the eight gifts of service to the body), Equipping gifts at the bottom (the two gifts that build up the body), with the two Charismatic gifts set apart as their own small constellation to the right.

Faint Champagne Gold lines connect related gifts based on the pairings identified in each gift's body-application section. These lines fade in on initial load, suggesting the constellation is revealing itself to the user rather than being presented. The lines are not decorative -- they are theology. They show that gifts function in clusters, never in isolation.

### 5.3 Interaction Model

**On hover (desktop) / tap (mobile):**
- The gift's point of light expands gently (subtle glow animation)
- A preview panel appears next to the point -- gift name in Michroma uppercase, essence statement in Cormorant Garamond italic, primary scripture reference in Champagne Gold
- The gifts that pair with this one show a subtle highlight on their connecting lines, hinting at the relationships

**On click/tap:**
- The full gift profile opens (modal overlay on desktop, full-screen view on mobile)
- Shows: gift name and category eyebrow, essence statement, working definition, definition anchor scripture, manifestation witnesses with notes, stewardship charge, body-application (all four subsections), and the formation note
- Does NOT show the assessment questions themselves (those belong to the assessment, not the exploration)
- Includes a "See related gifts" footer showing the gift's pairings as clickable links
- Includes a persistent footer CTA: "Ready to discover where the Spirit is at work in you? Begin the assessment →"

**Category navigation:**
- Subtle Champagne Gold eyebrow labels above each category cluster: MANIFESTATION, MINISTRY, EQUIPPING, CHARISMATIC
- Hovering over a category label dims the other categories slightly, focusing attention on the selected one
- Mobile uses a horizontal scroll between categories with the labels as anchor points

### 5.4 The Essence Statements

Each gift receives a single sentence -- 12 to 18 words -- that captures the gift's character. This is the line that appears on hover, the line that anchors the full profile, and the line a user would screenshot or share. These statements function the same way the Fruit assessment's formation statements function: theologically substantive, instantly memorable, unmistakably Counter Formation in voice.

All 19 essence statements are drafted in the gift entries (Section 7).

### 5.5 The CTA Flow

The Constellation lives within the assessment intro screen, not as a separate route. The intro screen presents two clear paths:

**Primary CTA (gold border, prominent):** `BEGIN ASSESSMENT`
**Secondary path (lighter weight, beneath):** `Explore the gifts first →`

Selecting "Explore the gifts first" opens the Constellation as a full-page experience. The Constellation always carries a persistent CTA at the bottom of the screen returning users to the assessment when they're ready. Users can move freely between exploration and assessment.

### 5.6 Technical Notes for Claude Code

- Component name suggestion: `GiftConstellation.jsx`
- Lives within the assessment route: `/field-guide/gifts` (the assessment intro), with the Constellation as a state within that route rather than a separate URL
- Uses GSAP for the connection-line reveal animations (consistent with existing CF site animations)
- Uses Framer Motion (or equivalent) for the hover/tap expand and modal transitions
- The full gift profile modal uses the existing ScriptureRef component for all scripture references
- On mobile, the Constellation reflows to a vertical layout grouped by category, with the connection lines simplified or omitted; the same hover/tap interactions apply with adjusted positioning
- All gift data is pulled from `src/data/gifts/` -- one JSON file per gift (`wisdom.json`, `knowledge.json`, etc.), exported in biblical order through `src/data/gifts/index.js`. Each gift entry contains: key, name, category, essenceStatement, workingDefinition, diagnosticChallenge, scriptureAnchor, scriptureAnchorText, manifestationWitnesses (array), stewardshipCharge, inclinationQuestions, fruitfulnessQuestion, communityConfirmationQuestion, bodyApplication (object with where, whatItLooksLike, distortions, pairings), pairsWith (array of gift keys), formationOutput (object with active, anchorScripture, anchorScriptureText, weeklyPractice, ruleOfLifeCrosslink), and edgeCases. Consumers import via `import { gifts, giftsByKey, giftsByCategory } from '../data/gifts'`.
- The pairing relationships rendered as constellation lines come from the `pairsWith` field on each gift -- these must be defined symmetrically (if Teaching pairs with Shepherding, Shepherding must also pair with Teaching). Symmetry is enforced at import time by the validator in `src/data/gifts/index.js`, which throws on any asymmetric pairing.

### 5.7 Constellation Pairing Map

The following pairings are rendered as gold connecting lines in the Constellation. Each pairing is biblically defensible and reflects the way these gifts naturally cluster in the body.

**Manifestation pairings:**
- Wisdom ↔ Knowledge (revelatory gifts that function together)
- Wisdom ↔ Discernment (the gifts of spiritual sight)
- Knowledge ↔ Prophecy (revelatory truth, declared and applied)
- Faith ↔ Healing (trust that opens the way for the Spirit's power)
- Faith ↔ Miracles (the same dynamic in larger demonstration)
- Prophecy ↔ Discernment (the gift and its accountability check)
- Healing ↔ Miracles (often co-manifest in the same person)

**Ministry pairings:**
- Teaching ↔ Shepherding (truth and the heart that delivers it)
- Teaching ↔ Exhortation (instruction and the call forward into application)
- Exhortation ↔ Mercy (the call forward and the comfort that holds the wound)
- Serving ↔ Hospitality (the practical expressions of care)
- Serving ↔ Administration (the gift and the structure that enables it)
- Giving ↔ Mercy (resources directed toward the suffering)
- Leadership ↔ Administration (vision and execution)
- Leadership ↔ Shepherding (the dual call to direct and care)

**Equipping pairings:**
- Evangelism ↔ Teaching (gospel proclamation that requires depth)
- Shepherding ↔ Teaching (already noted -- the most theologically essential pairing)

**Charismatic pairings:**
- Tongues ↔ Interpretation of Tongues (Paul's explicit instruction in 1 Cor 14)

These pairings appear symmetrically in the data file -- if Teaching's `pairsWith` includes Shepherding, Shepherding's `pairsWith` must include Teaching.

## 7.1 -- WISDOM

### GIFT 1 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Wisdom

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given utterance of God's wisdom into a specific situation that natural prudence cannot reach.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to speak God's wisdom into a specific situation -- not the general practical wisdom available to any thoughtful believer, but the moment-specific, situation-specific utterance that the Spirit places on the lips of his servant. Wisdom as a gift differs from wisdom as a general Christian virtue: every believer is called to seek wisdom (James 1:5), but the gift of wisdom is the Spirit's particular empowerment to speak the right word at the right moment in the right way -- often when the natural mind would not have arrived at it.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is easily confused with general intelligence, life experience, or natural prudence. The person who reads widely, listens well, and processes carefully will often give counsel that is helpful and good -- without ever functioning in the gift of wisdom. The gift's signature is that the wisdom did not come from the person's own resources. They surprise themselves with what they say. The hearer recognizes that the word addresses something the speaker could not have known on their own.

### Definition Anchor

> *"For to one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:8

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"But they could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which he was speaking."*
> -- Acts 6:10

*Stephen before the Sanhedrin -- the gift of wisdom in confrontation. His opponents were trained scholars and skilled debaters, but Stephen's wisdom was not human in origin. Luke records that they "could not withstand" it, then immediately ties it to the Spirit. The gift produces utterance that exceeds the speaker's natural capacity.*

> *"After they finished speaking, James replied, 'Brothers, listen to me... my judgment is that we should not trouble those of the Gentiles who turn to God...'"*
> -- Acts 15:13, 19

*James at the Jerusalem Council -- the gift of wisdom applied to a specific crisis. The early church faced a question that could have split it permanently. James's Spirit-given wisdom found a path that honored scripture, kept the gospel free of legalism, and held the body together. The gift functions here as discernment in motion -- not just truth, but the right truth at the right moment for the right body.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere."*
> -- James 3:17

*The diagnostic test for wisdom from God is the wisdom's character, not just its accuracy. Pure, peaceable, gentle, merciful, impartial. A "wise" word that produces division, sharpness, or pride is not from above, regardless of how technically correct it may be.*

### Inclination Questions

1. When someone brings me a complex situation, I often find myself saying something I had not thought of in advance -- a phrase, a question, or a redirection that addresses what they actually needed rather than what they asked.

2. People in conflict or transition come to me for counsel before they go to others, and what I offer tends to bring clarity rather than additional options to consider.

3. I sense that what I say in moments of counsel exceeds my own thinking on the subject -- I find myself saying the right thing without having worked out beforehand why it was the right thing.

### Fruitfulness Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

When you have offered counsel or insight into a difficult situation, how often have you seen visible evidence afterward that the word produced clarity, peace, or a wise decision -- rather than the person remaining stuck or pursuing their original course unchanged?

### Community-Confirmation Question (Trusted Person)

*5-point frequency scale, plus "I haven't been in a position to see this" option*

When [Name] speaks into a difficult or complex situation, what they say tends to cut through to the heart of the matter -- producing clarity or peace that people couldn't reach on their own.

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Wisdom functions in elder boards, pastoral counseling, leadership discernment moments, family crisis conversations, mentoring relationships, conflict mediation, and major decision points. It often appears in people who hold no formal title -- the trusted older saint in the back of the sanctuary whom the pastor seeks out before making a hard call. The gift surfaces in moments when a body or family or leader is at an impasse.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose word into a complex situation consistently produces clarity rather than more confusion. The person whose counsel tends to bring peace even when the answer is hard. A wisdom-gifted believer functioning well does not solve problems by force of intellect; they speak something that lands in the situation as if it had been waiting for that exact word. The hearer's response is often "I should have seen that" -- not because it was obvious, but because the wisdom revealed what was already there.

**Common distortions.**
This gift can become a self-styled oracle role -- the person who pronounces on every situation whether asked or not. It can become detached intellectualism that distances the wisdom-giver from the actual cost the hearer is paying. It can become tribal wisdom: applying scripture or Christian principle accurately to "their" people while being blind to its application to one's own tribe. The most dangerous distortion is when the gift becomes prestige -- the wisdom-giver values being sought out over the actual care of those who seek them.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Wisdom functions alongside **Knowledge** (the two revelatory gifts that Paul lists together in 1 Corinthians 12:8) and **Discernment of Spirits** (the gift that distinguishes the source of an impression). A wisdom-gifted believer without discernment can speak with confidence into a situation whose actual dynamics they have not seen. A discerner without wisdom may see clearly but lack the right word to address what they see.

### Formation Output for Active Wisdom

*Displayed when composite score is at or above 70 with confirmation present.*

The Spirit's work through you in wisdom is to speak God's word into specific situations in ways the situation's own logic cannot produce. This is what Stephen exhibited before the Sanhedrin and what James exhibited at Jerusalem -- not the application of stored knowledge, but the Spirit-given utterance that addresses the actual heart of a matter when wisdom is needed.

The stewardship of this gift requires constant return to its source. James 3:17 is the diagnostic test: the wisdom is recognizable by what it produces -- purity, peace, gentleness, impartiality, mercy. When your counsel begins to produce sharpness, division, or your own prestige, the gift has been cut off from its source and is now functioning out of your own resources. The wisdom-gifted believer who stops praying and stops listening to scripture becomes a confident speaker with depleted authority -- and the body suffers from words that sound right but are no longer the Spirit's.

#### Anchor Scripture

> *"If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him."*
> -- James 1:5

#### Weekly Practice

This week, when someone brings you a situation that calls for counsel, do not respond immediately. Take an extra beat -- thirty seconds, a sentence of prayer in your mind, a deliberate pause before you speak. Ask the Spirit for the right word before you offer your own. Notice what changes in what you say. The gift of wisdom does not bypass the practice of seeking God; it is fed by it.

#### Rule of Life Crosslink
This gift connects to the **Prayer rhythm** in your Rule of Life. Wisdom is fed by sustained, listening prayer. The wisdom-gifted believer who stops asking becomes a confident counselor speaking from depleted reserves.

### Edge Cases

#### Emerging Wisdom
*Displayed when composite score is 50-69, or when inclination and fruitfulness are high but no community confirmation has come in.*

Your responses suggest the Spirit may be developing wisdom in you, but it has not yet produced the consistent fruit of clarity in others, or the people closest to you have not yet seen it function. This is an invitation, not a verdict. Wisdom is a gift that grows through use under accountability. Find a trusted, more seasoned believer in your life and offer to sit under their counsel before offering your own. Take counsel before you give it. Then ask, after a season, whether what you offer in moments of decision is bringing clarity to others.

#### Inclination-Confirmation Gap
*Displayed when the user scores high on inclination toward wisdom, but the trusted people who responded scored low on confirmation.*

You sense the Spirit working through you in wisdom, but the people who know you well have not seen it manifest as clearly as you have. This is worth taking seriously. The gift of wisdom is one of the easiest to overestimate in oneself because internal confidence can be mistaken for Spirit-given utterance. Submit your sense of this gift to a process of testing. When you next offer counsel, ask the person afterward -- gently, without pressure -- what they actually heard you say and what they did with it. The Spirit's wisdom typically leaves a trail of clarity in others that they themselves recognize.

#### Confirmation-Inclination Gap
*Displayed when trusted people score high on confirmation of wisdom, but the user did not claim it as a primary inclination.*

Two or more of the people who weighed in see wisdom in you that you did not claim for yourself. This is significant. The gift of wisdom is often more evident to those who receive it than to those who carry it -- the wisdom-gifted believer rarely thinks of themselves as wise. Worth praying about. Worth taking seriously when someone brings you a complex situation in the coming weeks -- not by assuming you have the answer, but by listening carefully to what the Spirit places on your lips and noticing what it does in the hearer.

#### Quiet Wisdom
*Displayed when composite score is below 50.*

Wisdom is not where the Spirit is most visibly working through you in this season. That is information, not deficiency. Every believer is called to seek wisdom and to pray for it; James 1:5 applies to all. But the specific gift of wisdom -- the Spirit-given utterance that addresses moments of decision and crisis -- is not your current area of contribution. Trust the wisdom-gifted believers around you when complex situations arise, and look to the gifts where the Spirit is more clearly at work in you. The Spirit distributes his gifts as he wills, and the picture you receive today is a picture of this season -- not a permanent verdict. What is Quiet now may become Active in a later season as the Spirit moves and your ministry context shifts.

## 7.2 -- KNOWLEDGE

### GIFT 2 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Knowledge

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given utterance of knowledge that natural means could not have produced.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given utterance of knowledge that exceeds what the speaker could have known through natural means. The gift of knowledge in Paul's list is distinct from learned knowledge or theological study. ### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is easy to confuse with intuition, careful observation, or theological learning. The gift's signature is the same as wisdom's: the knowledge did not come from the believer's own resources. They surprise themselves with what they know. ## 7.3 -- FAITH

### GIFT 3 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Faith

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given confidence to trust God against the evidence when others cannot yet see what God will do.*

### Working Definition
A special, Spirit-given measure of confidence in God that goes beyond the saving faith all believers share. The gift of faith is the empowerment to trust God in specific moments when the circumstances would warrant fear, doubt, or retreat. It is not a personality trait of being naturally optimistic. It is the Spirit's gift of conviction at the moment when conviction is needed -- to step out where most would not, to persevere when others would quit, to believe God for something specific that natural calculation would call foolish.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is often confused with personality optimism, denial of difficulty, or strong willpower. The naturally hopeful person, the believer who refuses to acknowledge hard realities, and the determined leader can all be mistaken as "people of great faith" when their disposition has no necessary connection to the Spirit's gift. The signature of this gift is that the believer's confidence is in God specifically -- not in the outcome, not in themselves, not in the situation's eventual reversal -- and that their confidence has produced visible action that others have benefited from.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another faith by the same Spirit..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:9

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"Peter answered him, 'Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.' He said, 'Come.' So Peter got out of the boat and walked on the water and came to Jesus."*
> -- Matthew 14:28-29

*Peter walking on water -- the prototypical picture of the gift of faith. The other disciples watched. Peter alone received the special measure of faith to act on Jesus's word. The gift's signature is not that Peter never sank; it is that he stepped out of the boat in the first place. The gift produces action, not just internal conviction.*

> *"So take heart, men, for I have faith in God that it will be exactly as I have been told."*
> -- Acts 27:25

*Paul on the storm-tossed ship. The crew had given up; Paul had not. The gift of faith here is not a vague hopefulness but a specific confidence in a specific word from God. It produced calm in chaos and preserved the lives of 276 men. The gift is given for moments when the body or those around the believer need confidence that the believer cannot produce from themselves.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"Now faith is the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen... And without faith it is impossible to please him, for whoever would draw near to God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him."*
> -- Hebrews 11:1, 6

*The gift of faith is fed by the same root as the saving faith of every believer: assurance, conviction, and active drawing near to God. The faith-gifted believer who stops drawing near loses the source. The gift cannot be sustained on past assurance; it requires continued seeking.*

## 7.4 -- HEALING

### GIFT 4 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Healing

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit's power working through the believer to bring healing where natural means cannot reach.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given empowerment for the believer to be the conduit through whom God brings healing -- physical, emotional, spiritual, or relational. Paul's phrase in 1 Corinthians 12:9 is plural ("gifts of healings"), suggesting that the gift is not a single, generalized power but a series of specific Spirit-given moments where healing flows through the believer. The healing belongs to God; the gift is the believer's role in the moment.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is one of the most contested in contemporary Christian practice, and the contention has produced two opposite errors: the dismissal of the gift as having ceased, and the abuse of the gift through claims that any believer with enough faith can heal at will. Both miss Paul's framing. The gift is given by the Spirit according to his will. It is real. It is rare. It does not function as formula. The diagnostic question is whether the believer has seen real, verifiable healing flow through them in moments where they prayed or ministered in obedience to the Spirit's leading.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another gifts of healing by the one Spirit..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:9

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"And Peter directed his gaze at him, as did John, and said, 'Look at us.' And he fixed his attention on them, expecting to receive something from them. > -- Acts 3:4-7

*Peter and John at the Beautiful Gate -- the apostolic pattern for healing as a Spirit-given gift. Peter did not heal on his own authority. > -- Acts 9:33-34

*Peter and Aeneas -- the same pattern in a non-apostolic context. The healing was attributed explicitly to Jesus, not to Peter. The gift functions when the believer becomes the means through whom God acts, never the source of the power itself.*

> -- James 5:14-15

*The biblical pattern for healing is communal, accountable, and tied to repentance and prayer. It is not the lone "healer" performing for crowds. It is the elders, the oil, the prayer of faith, the gathered body. The gift functions best within the structure scripture describes.*

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Healing functions in prayer ministry contexts, hospital and bedside visits, inner-healing and counseling ministry, deliverance prayer, missionary contexts where access to medical care is limited, and corporate prayer in church settings. It often appears in believers who have a quiet, sustained prayer life and are willing to be present in suffering rather than performing for crowds.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer who prays for the sick with confidence in God but without claims about their own power. The one who carries the prayer for the suffering family member for years and sees fruit slowly emerge. A healing-gifted believer functioning well points consistently to Christ, not to themselves; they take no credit and refuse no failure. They are at peace when healing comes and at peace when it does not. They understand that the gift is the Spirit's, and the timing is God's.

This gift is uniquely vulnerable to abuse, and the contemporary church has provided ample evidence of how. ## 7.5 -- MIRACLES

### GIFT 5 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Miracles

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit's power producing acts that exceed the ordinary course of nature, given for the body's good and the glory of God.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given empowerment for the believer to be the means through which God acts in ways that exceed the ordinary course of nature. Paul's word in 1 Corinthians 12:10 is *energemata dynameon* -- "workings of powers." The gift is broader than healing and encompasses any moment when the Spirit's power produces what natural means cannot. As with healing, the power is God's; the gift is the believer's role. The gift is real, attested throughout church history, and never formulaic.

### Diagnostic Challenge
The contemporary church has split sharply on this gift. Cessationist traditions have argued that the miraculous gifts ceased with the apostolic era. Charismatic traditions have sometimes inflated normal answered prayer into "miracle" language, diluting the category. The instrument's position is the continuationist one: miracles continue, they are rare, they are sovereign, and they cannot be produced by technique or formula. The diagnostic question is whether the believer has been the means through which acts have occurred that cannot be explained by ordinary providence -- and whether those acts have produced glory for God and good for the body.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another the working of miracles..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:10

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"But Peter put them all outside, and knelt down and prayed; and turning to the body he said, 'Tabitha, arise.' And she opened her eyes, and when she saw Peter she sat up... He gave her his hand and raised her up. Then, calling the saints and widows, he presented her alive."*
> -- Acts 9:40-41

*Peter and Tabitha -- the gift of miracles in its sharpest biblical form: the dead raised. Peter knelt and prayed before speaking; the action followed the seeking of God. The miracle confirmed the gospel in Joppa and brought many to faith. The gift functions for the body's good and God's glory, not for the spectacle of the miracle itself.*

## 7.6 -- PROPHECY

### GIFT 6 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Prophecy

### Essence Statement
*A specific word from God, given for the strengthening, comfort, and direction of his people.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to receive and communicate a specific word from God for the upbuilding, encouragement, and consolation of his people (1 Corinthians 14:3). New Testament prophecy is distinct from Old Testament prophetic office. The Old Testament prophet spoke with absolute, inerrant authority -- a misspoken word from a prophet was a capital offense. New Testament prophecy is fallible, must be tested, and is given for the strengthening of the church rather than for the establishment of new doctrine. The gift is real and active in the church today, but it operates under the authority of scripture and within the accountability of the body.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is the most theologically contested of all the gifts addressed in this assessment. Cessationist traditions argue it has ceased; continuationist traditions affirm its continuation but disagree sharply about how it operates. The instrument's position follows the framework of Sam Storms and Wayne Grudem: New Testament prophecy is fallible, subject to testing, and given for the body's strengthening. The diagnostic question is whether the believer has received specific, Spirit-given impressions that they have shared, and whether those impressions have proven accurate, helpful, and confirmed by mature believers in their community.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another prophecy, to another the ability to distinguish between spirits..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:10

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"Now in these days prophets came down from Jerusalem to Antioch. And one of them named Agabus stood up and foretold by the Spirit that there would be a great famine over all the world (this took place in the days of Claudius). So the disciples determined, every one according to his ability, to send relief to the brothers living in Judea."*
> -- Acts 11:27-29

*Agabus prophesying the famine -- specific, accurate, and immediately actionable. The prophecy did not produce passive reception; it produced concrete response. The church mobilized financially to prepare for what God had revealed. The gift functions when it produces the body's obedience to a Spirit-given direction.*

> *"The one who prophesies speaks to people for their upbuilding and encouragement and consolation."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:3

*Paul's working definition of the gift's purpose. Three functions: building up, calling forward, comforting. Any "prophecy" that does not produce one of these outcomes -- that tears down, that condemns without redemption, that introduces fear or shame -- is failing the diagnostic test Paul gives. The gift is for the body's good.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"Do not quench the Spirit. Do not despise prophecies, but test everything; hold fast what is good."*
> -- 1 Thessalonians 5:19-21

*Paul's command is balanced. Do not despise the gift -- which means do not dismiss it categorically. But test everything -- which means do not receive prophecy uncritically. The gift functions within the testing community of the body, never independently of it.*

3. I have learned to wait, test, and pray about what I sense before sharing it, and I am willing to be wrong, to be corrected, and to submit what I have received to my pastor or trusted, mature believers before bringing it to the person it concerns.

*5-point frequency scale, plus "I haven't been in a position to see this" option*

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Prophecy functions in prayer ministry contexts, corporate worship settings where prophetic words are weighed by leadership before being shared, small group prayer, intercessory prayer for missionaries and ministries, and pastoral counseling. It often manifests as a specific impression for a specific person, given for their strengthening or direction. The gift is also exercised in scripture-grounded teaching that addresses the body with prophetic edge -- the preacher whose sermon names what the congregation is actually facing in a way that could only have come from the Spirit's direction.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose prophetic impressions produce visible fruit -- people strengthened, encouraged, consoled, redirected. The one who submits what they receive to testing before sharing. A prophetically-gifted believer functioning well is humble about their own impressions, eager for correction, careful with words, and consistent in pointing the recipient back to Christ. They are slower to speak than to listen, and the words they do share carry weight precisely because they are not casual with the gift.

**Common distortions.**
The gift of prophecy has produced more damage in the modern church than perhaps any other when misused. It can become a tool for control -- "the Lord told me you are to..." used to manipulate or direct others without accountability. It can become spiritual abuse, where unverified words are wielded against people's choices and identities. It can become spectacle, with prophetic ministries built on dramatic public delivery rather than fruitful body-building. It can become rote -- generic "words" that lack specificity and produce no testable confirmation. The most dangerous distortion is the elevation of New Testament prophecy to the level of Old Testament prophetic authority, where the prophet's word is treated as inerrant and disagreement is framed as resistance to God.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Prophecy functions alongside **Discernment of Spirits** -- the gift Paul names in the very next phrase of 1 Corinthians 12:10. The pairing is theological: prophecy must be tested, and discernment is what tests it. A prophetically-gifted believer without access to discernment-gifted believers operates without the accountability check the New Testament demands. The gift also pairs with **Knowledge**, where specific Spirit-given information becomes a spoken word for a person or situation.

## 7.7 -- DISCERNMENT OF SPIRITS

### GIFT 7 OF 17 -- MANIFESTATION GIFTS

# Discernment of Spirits

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given sight to distinguish what is of God, what is of the flesh, and what is of the enemy.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given ability to distinguish between what is of God, what is of the natural human spirit, and what is of the demonic. The gift operates in moments of decision, prayer ministry, evaluation of teaching, and assessment of spiritual movements. It is not the same as critical thinking or general spiritual sensitivity. It is the Spirit's specific empowerment to perceive what is actually at work in a person, a teaching, or a situation -- and to do so with accuracy that natural assessment cannot reach.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is easily confused with general theological discernment, intuition, or suspicion. The believer who has been hurt by spiritual abuse may develop a sharp critical instinct that resembles discernment but is actually self-protection. The believer who is theologically well-trained may discern false teaching by application of doctrine rather than by Spirit-given sight. Both are valuable; neither is necessarily the gift. The gift's signature is the perception of what could not have been perceived through natural means -- the recognition that something is not what it appears, often before the evidence is visible.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another the ability to distinguish between spirits..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:10

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"But Peter said, 'Ananias, why has Satan filled your heart to lie to the Holy Spirit and to keep back for yourself part of the proceeds of the land?'"*
> -- Acts 5:3

*Peter discerning the deception of Ananias -- the gift's most acute manifestation. Peter perceived not only the lie but the source of the lie. He named "Satan" as the one behind the deception. The gift in operation goes beyond detecting falsehood; it perceives the spiritual reality animating the falsehood.*

> *"As we were going to the place of prayer, we were met by a slave girl who had a spirit of divination... She followed Paul and us, crying out, 'These men are servants of the Most High God, who proclaim to you the way of salvation.' And this she kept doing for many days. Paul, having become greatly annoyed, turned and said to the spirit, 'I command you in the name of Jesus Christ to come out of her.'"*
> -- Acts 16:16-18

*Paul at Philippi -- the gift discerning a spirit even when the spirit was speaking truth. The slave girl's words were technically accurate. Paul discerned that the source was demonic, and the discernment produced action: the spirit was cast out and the girl was freed. The gift sees beneath the surface, even when the surface appears to confirm what is good.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, for many false prophets have gone out into the world."*
> -- 1 John 4:1

*John's command names the gift's purpose: testing. The gift exists not for the believer's own protection but for the body's. Discernment is the New Testament's check against deception, and the church is more vulnerable now than ever to misled teaching, misused authority, and movements that appear spiritual but are not from God.*

3. I am drawn to questions of what is truly of God versus what is counterfeit, and I find that my assessment of teachers, ministries, and movements tends to age well -- what I have flagged as problematic has been borne out by later events.

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Discernment functions in elder boards, prayer ministry, evaluation of new teaching and curricula, missions and church planting decisions, response to spiritual movements, and pastoral counseling situations involving spiritual oppression. It also operates in less formal moments -- the believer who senses that a friendship is moving in a direction that is not of God, the parent who perceives that a child is being pulled toward something destructive, the small group leader who notices that a member's "growth" is actually performance.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose discernment proves consistently accurate over time. The one whose flags about teachers or movements turn out to have been warranted, often before others saw the problem. A discernment-gifted believer functioning well does not act primarily from suspicion; they perceive with sorrow what is not of God and respond with prayer and appropriate action. They are slow to speak, careful with what they share, and humble about their perceptions -- not loudly suspicious, but quietly accurate.

**Common distortions.**
This gift, when distorted, produces some of the most damaging dynamics in the church. It can become weaponized suspicion -- the believer who flags everyone and everything as suspect, who finds demonic influence everywhere, who treats their own impressions as inerrant judgment. It can become harshness toward genuine works of God that operate differently than the discerner expects. It can become tribal: the gift used to validate one's own community while finding fault with others. The most damaging distortion is the conflation of personal preference or theological tribe with Spirit-given discernment, where the believer's own bias is dressed up as God's verdict.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Discernment functions alongside **Prophecy** (the testing of prophetic impressions is one of discernment's primary services) and **Wisdom** (which provides the right word and the right response to what discernment has perceived). ## 7.8 -- TEACHING

### GIFT 8 OF 17 -- MINISTRY GIFTS

# Teaching

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit's work to make the truth of God land in a way the hearer cannot reach alone.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to communicate scripture, theology, or truth in a way that produces understanding and spiritual growth in others. Teaching as a gift is distinct from natural communication skill. Many thoughtful people can explain things clearly without the Spirit's specific empowerment to build up the body. Teaching as a gift produces transformation, not just comprehension -- the hearers walk away changed by what they have heard, not merely informed.

### Diagnostic Challenge
Teaching is the gift most often confused with natural ability. Plenty of intelligent, articulate people can explain things well without ever functioning in this gift. The questions have to separate "I'm good at explaining things" from "when I teach, the Spirit moves and people grow." The disconfirmation language built into each question filters for the Spirit-given dimension that produces visible spiritual fruit, not just understanding.

### Definition Anchor

> *"Having gifts that differ according to the grace given to us, let us use them: ... the one who teaches, in his teaching."*

## 7.9 -- EXHORTATION

### GIFT 9 OF 17 -- MINISTRY GIFTS

# Exhortation

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given capacity to come alongside another and call them forward into what God is doing in their life.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to encourage, strengthen, and call others forward in faith. The Greek word *parakaleō* carries the sense of "calling alongside" -- it is the same word used for the Holy Spirit as Paraclete. Exhortation is the human reflection of that work: the believer who comes alongside another and helps them see what God is doing, what God is calling them to, and what is true about them in Christ. Exhortation is distinct from teaching (which builds understanding) and from mercy (which holds the wound); it is the gift that produces movement forward.

### Diagnostic Challenge
Exhortation is often confused with cheerleading, motivational language, or a generally positive disposition. The naturally encouraging person may make others feel good without ever functioning in the gift. The signature of the gift is that the exhortation produces actual movement in the recipient -- a renewed step of obedience, a return to a discipline they had abandoned, a willingness to face what they had been avoiding. Exhortation that does not call forward is not exhortation in the New Testament sense.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...the one who exhorts, in his exhortation..."*
> -- Romans 12:8

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"Now Joseph, who was also called by the apostles Barnabas (which means son of encouragement), a Levite, a native of Cyprus, sold a field that belonged to him and brought the money and laid it at the apostles' feet... But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles..."*
> -- Acts 4:36-37, 9:27

*Barnabas -- the New Testament's named example of this gift. The apostles called him "son of encouragement" because of how he functioned. When Paul came to Jerusalem after his conversion and the disciples were afraid of him, Barnabas vouched for him. When the church at Antioch needed strengthening, Barnabas was sent. When John Mark failed and Paul was finished with him, Barnabas took him back and restored him. The gift produces forward motion in others -- often when no one else can see the way forward.*

> *"And let us consider how to stir up one another to love and good works, not neglecting to meet together, as is the habit of some, but encouraging one another, and all the more as you see the Day drawing near."*
> -- Hebrews 10:24-25

*The author of Hebrews describes the rhythm of exhortation as a communal discipline. The body is meant to stir one another up. Exhortation as a gift is the Spirit's particular empowerment of certain believers to do this work with unusual effectiveness, but the call to encourage one another belongs to every Christian.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called 'today,' that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin."*
> -- Hebrews 3:13

*The urgency in the stewardship is striking. Exhortation is given as a daily counter to the hardening effect of sin. The exhortation-gifted believer is meant to be a steady, present voice -- not a dramatic intervention but a sustained presence that keeps the body soft toward God.*

1. When I am with someone who is discouraged, stuck, or losing heart, I find that I can speak into their situation in ways that produce visible movement -- they leave the conversation more able to take the next step than they were when it began.

2. People in my life tend to come to me at decision points or in moments of struggle, and what they leave with is a renewed willingness to keep going, not just sympathy for what they are facing.

3. I find myself naturally calling people forward -- toward the obedience they have been avoiding, the conversation they need to have, the discipline they have neglected -- in a way they receive rather than resist.

### Fruitfulness Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Exhortation functions in small group leadership, mentoring relationships, pastoral care, recovery ministries, men's and women's ministry contexts, coaching and discipleship roles, and informal one-on-one conversations. It often appears in believers who are sought out for "advice" but whose real ministry is helping the other person see what they could not see on their own. The gift surfaces most strongly when someone is at a decision point or has lost momentum in their walk with God.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose presence in a struggling person's life consistently produces movement -- not because they push or guilt or manipulate, but because they help the person see what God is doing and what they need to do. The mentor whose phone call you take when you are about to quit. The friend who calls forward the obedience you had been avoiding. An exhortation-gifted believer functioning well sees the person clearly, names what is true about them in Christ, and calls them into the next faithful step -- without flattery, without harshness, without taking responsibility for the outcome.

**Common distortions.**
This gift can become mere cheerleading -- words that make people feel good without producing change. It can become manipulation: using language of "calling forward" to push people in directions that serve the exhorter's agenda rather than God's work in them. It can become impatient: rushing people through grief, pain, or genuine struggle into premature action. The most subtle distortion is when exhortation becomes about the exhorter's identity -- the believer who needs to be the encourager, who needs to be needed, who derives their sense of self from being the one who calls others forward.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Exhortation functions alongside **Mercy** -- the gift that holds the wound while exhortation calls forward. Without mercy, exhortation becomes impatient and forces movement that people are not yet ready for. It also pairs with **Teaching**, which provides the substance that exhortation calls people to live out, and with **Shepherding**, which provides the long-term care that sustained exhortation requires.

## 7.10 -- SERVING (HELPS)

### GIFT 10 OF 17 -- MINISTRY GIFTS

# Serving (Helps)

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given capacity to meet practical needs that free others to function in the gifts God has given them.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given inclination and ability to meet practical needs in the body of Christ in ways that enable other gifts to function. Paul uses two related words: *diakonia* in Romans 12:7 (service) and *antilempsis* in 1 Corinthians 12:28 (helps -- literally "taking hold alongside"). The gift addresses the practical infrastructure of the body's life: the meal delivered to the new mother, the chairs set up before the gathering, the financial books kept accurate, the building cleaned, the website maintained. Without this gift in operation, the body cannot sustain its more visible ministries.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is the most undervalued of the ministry gifts in contemporary church culture. It is also the most easily mistaken for either general helpfulness or a personality preference for behind-the-scenes work. The naturally helpful person may serve without ever functioning in the gift; the introvert who avoids visible ministry may serve from preference rather than calling. The signature of the gift is that the serving produces visible flourishing in the body -- other gifts are able to function because of what the serving believer has done, and the body's life is sustained by their faithful contribution.

### Definition Anchor

> *"And God has appointed in the church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then miracles, then gifts of healing, helping, administrating, and various kinds of tongues."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:28

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"I commend to you our sister Phoebe, a servant of the church at Cenchreae, that you may welcome her in the Lord in a way worthy of the saints, and help her in whatever she may need from you, for she has been a patron of many and of myself as well."*
> -- Romans 16:1-2

*Phoebe, the servant of the church at Cenchreae -- Paul's specific commendation of a servant of the body whose work was substantial enough that he names her in his closing. The gift is dignified and apostolic, not lower-status work. Phoebe's serving enabled Paul's mission and the church's life.*

> *"May the Lord grant mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chains, but when he arrived in Rome he searched for me earnestly and found me -- may the Lord grant him to find mercy from the Lord on that Day! -- and you well know all the service he rendered at Ephesus."*

*Onesiphorus -- the believer who refreshed Paul in prison and rendered service the wider church remembered. The gift produces relief, refreshment, and the sustenance of others in ministry. > *"As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace... whoever serves, as one who serves by the strength that God supplies -- in order that in everything God may be glorified through Jesus Christ."*
> -- 1 Peter 4:10-11

*Peter ties the gift's exercise directly to the strength God supplies. The serving believer is not running on their own resources; they are functioning by the grace that God provides. This is the corrective against the burnout that can attach to this gift: the strength comes from God, and serving in his strength produces glory rather than exhaustion.*

**Where it shows up.**

## 7.11 -- GIVING

### GIFT 11 OF 17 -- MINISTRY GIFTS

# Giving

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given capacity to fund the Kingdom with extraordinary generosity, cheerfulness, and discernment.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to direct resources -- financial, material, or otherwise -- toward Kingdom purposes with unusual generosity, cheerfulness, and discernment about where the giving will produce fruit. The gift of giving is not the same as tithing or general Christian generosity, both of which are called for in every believer. The gift is a particular empowerment to give beyond what natural calculation would warrant, often quietly, and with a Spirit-given sense of where the giving needs to land. Paul's word in Romans 12:8 carries the connotation of giving "with simplicity" or "with generous purity" -- without ulterior motive, without need for recognition.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is easily confused with high income or generally generous personality. A wealthy believer may give substantially without functioning in the gift; a generous person may give from temperament rather than from Spirit-given empowerment. The signature of the gift is not the size of the gift but the cheerful, discerning, often sacrificial pattern of giving that produces visible Kingdom fruit. The gift may operate in believers of modest means as fully as in those with substantial resources -- the Macedonian churches' giving in 2 Corinthians 8 came out of "extreme poverty."

### Definition Anchor

> *"...the one who contributes, in generosity..."*
> -- Romans 12:8

## 7.13 -- MERCY

*The Spirit-given capacity to enter another's suffering in a way that brings them to God.*

The Spirit-given capacity to enter into the suffering of others and produce comfort, healing, or presence that brings them closer to God. The Greek word *eleeō* in Romans 12:8 carries the sense of compassionate action -- not just pity, but pity that responds. The gift of mercy is distinct from general kindness or sympathy. It is the believer's particular empowerment to be the means through whom God's compassion reaches the suffering, the marginalized, and the broken.

### Diagnostic Challenge
Mercy is easily confused with sympathy, emotional sensitivity, or a naturally caring disposition. The naturally compassionate person may sympathize with suffering without ever functioning in the gift; the empath may absorb others' pain without producing the comfort, healing, or movement toward God that the gift's fruit suggests. The signature of the gift is that mercy's presence in suffering brings the suffering person closer to God, not just closer to the merciful believer. The gift produces visible spiritual fruit -- people are changed by having been the recipient of Spirit-empowered mercy.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...the one who does acts of mercy, with cheerfulness..."*
> -- Romans 12:8

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"But a Samaritan, as he journeyed, came to where he was, and when he saw him, he had compassion. He went to him and bound up his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. Then he set him on his own animal and brought him to an inn and took care of him."*
> -- Luke 10:33-34

*The Good Samaritan -- Jesus's chosen example of mercy in action. The Samaritan saw, had compassion, and responded with concrete care. The gift of mercy in its biblical form is not abstract; it is the willingness to stop, to come close, to bind up, to pay the cost, to remain present in suffering. Mercy gets dirty.*

> *"But Ruth said, 'Do not urge me to leave you or to return from following you. For where you go I will go, and where you lodge I will lodge. Your people shall be my people, and your God my God.'"*
> -- Ruth 1:16

*Ruth and Naomi -- mercy as covenant commitment to the suffering. Naomi was bitter, displaced, widowed, and aging. Ruth chose her. The mercy here is not a single act but a long covenant of presence with someone in their lowest season. The gift, in its full form, includes the willingness to stay.*

> -- Matthew 5:7

*Mercy in Jesus's framing operates within the economy of grace -- those who show mercy receive it. The gift is sustained by being exercised, and the believer who stops showing mercy becomes harder, less able to receive what God continues to offer.*

## 7.14 -- HOSPITALITY

# Hospitality

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given capacity to welcome others into one's life in a way that creates spaces of belonging.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given capacity to welcome others -- particularly strangers, travelers, the new, and the lonely -- into one's home, table, or life in a way that produces real belonging. The Greek word *philoxenia* in Romans 12:13 means "love of strangers." The gift is distinct from general sociability or natural extroversion. It is the believer's particular empowerment to make others feel met, seen, and welcomed in spaces where they would otherwise feel out of place. The home, the table, and the time of the hospitality-gifted believer become a place where the body of Christ becomes visible.

Hospitality is easily confused with entertaining, social skill, or a love of hosting. The naturally warm person may care for those around them effectively without ever functioning in the gift; the trained counselor may listen well without the Spirit's empowerment for spiritual oversight. The signature of the gift is the long-term, sustained, costly care of specific people over years rather than encounters, with the goal of their growth in Christ as the orienting purpose of the care. Shepherds know their sheep, and the sheep know they are known.

### Definition Anchor

> *"And he gave the apostles, the prophets, the evangelists, the shepherds and teachers, to equip the saints for the work of ministry, for building up the body of Christ..."*
> -- Ephesians 4:11-12

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"I am the good shepherd. I know my own and my own know me, just as the Father knows me and I know the Father; and I lay down my life for the sheep."*
> -- John 10:14-15

*Jesus as the Good Shepherd -- the source and pattern of all Christian shepherding. The gift, exercised faithfully, mirrors what Jesus describes: knowing the sheep individually, being known by them, and being willing to bear the cost of their care. Every shepherd-gifted believer's ministry derives from and points back to the Chief Shepherd.*

> *"When they had finished breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, 'Simon, son of John, do you love me more than these?' He said to him, 'Yes, Lord; you know that I love you.' He said to him, 'Feed my lambs.'... He said to him a third time, 'Simon, son of John, do you love me?' Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, 'Do you love me?' and he said to him, 'Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.' Jesus said to him, 'Feed my sheep.'"*
> -- John 21:15, 17

*Jesus restoring Peter and commissioning him to shepherd. The threefold question matches Peter's threefold denial. The commission is not given to the impressive disciple but to the restored one. Shepherding as a gift is rooted in the shepherd's own restoration -- the believer who has been forgiven much shepherds with greater faithfulness because they have known the Chief Shepherd's care personally.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"Shepherd the flock of God that is among you, exercising oversight, not under compulsion, but willingly, as God would have you; not for shameful gain, but eagerly; not domineering over those in your charge, but being examples to the flock. And when the chief Shepherd appears, you will receive the unfading crown of glory."*
> -- 1 Peter 5:2-4

*Peter's instructions to elders -- the most direct stewardship charge for shepherds in scripture. Six diagnostic markers: willing (not compelled), eager (not greedy), exemplary (not domineering). The negative formulations matter as much as the positive ones. Shepherding can become its opposite -- compelled, greedy, domineering -- when the gift is exercised from the wrong motive or without accountability.*

### Inclination Questions

1. I find myself taking long-term responsibility for the spiritual lives of specific people -- knowing them individually, walking with them through seasons, and remaining present in their growth and their struggle over years rather than encounters.

2. People entrusted to my care -- in small groups, mentoring relationships, family, ministry contexts -- consistently grow in Christ, and the growth is sustained over time rather than dependent on the intensity of any single conversation.

3. I am willing to bear the cost of long-term shepherding -- the disappointment when those I shepherd struggle, the heaviness of carrying their burdens in prayer, the patience required when growth is slow -- and the cost has not extinguished my willingness to keep showing up.

### Fruitfulness Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

When you have shepherded specific people over a sustained period of time, how often have they grown into deeper maturity in Christ as a direct result of your care -- formed disciples who continue to walk faithfully, not just moments of intense connection that did not produce lasting growth?

### Community-Confirmation Question (Trusted Person)

*5-point frequency scale, plus "I haven't been in a position to see this" option*

When [Name] takes responsibility for someone's spiritual life -- in a small group, mentoring relationship, or other long-term context -- the person consistently grows in Christ over time, and they know they are genuinely known and cared for.

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
Shepherding functions in pastoral ministry, elder roles, small group leadership, discipleship mentoring, women's and men's ministry, recovery ministry, missions team leadership, and family discipleship. It also appears in informal contexts -- the older believer who consistently mentors specific younger believers, the parent who shepherds their children's faith with unusual care, the workplace believer who walks with specific coworkers through long seasons of growth. The gift surfaces wherever sustained, costly, long-term care of specific believers is required.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose oversight of specific people consistently produces growth in Christ over years. The one who knows the people under their care individually -- their stories, their struggles, their slow growth -- and remains present through the long seasons. A shepherding-gifted believer functioning well receives those under their care as a trust from the Chief Shepherd, not as a possession of their own. They feed the flock with scripture, protect against false teaching, retrieve the wandering, and remain present in the long, often slow work of formation. They give credit for growth to God and bear the weight of failure without despair.

**Common distortions.**
This gift can become possessive: the shepherd who treats the flock as their own, who is threatened by other voices speaking into the lives of those they shepherd, who builds dependence on themselves rather than on Christ. It can become controlling: oversight that drifts into domination, where the shepherd directs the lives of those under their care in ways that exceed the gift's proper scope. It can become spiritual abuse: the most damaging distortion, where shepherding authority is used to silence, manipulate, or harm those who have trusted the shepherd with their formation. Peter's negative formulations in 1 Peter 5 name the distortions directly: under compulsion, for shameful gain, domineering. Each of these is a real and present temptation for those who carry this gift.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Shepherding functions alongside **Teaching** (which provides the substance with which the flock is fed) -- this is the most theologically essential pairing of all the equipping gifts, and Ephesians 4:11 names the two together. It also pairs with **Mercy** (which sustains the shepherd in the long care of suffering sheep), with **Leadership** (which provides the direction that shepherding implements at the level of individual believers), and with **Exhortation** (which calls the sheep forward into faithful next steps).

## CHARISMATIC GIFTS -- FRAMING NOTE

What follows are the two charismatic gifts assessed in this instrument: Tongues and Interpretation of Tongues. Both are real, both are active in the church today, and both are addressed with a modified format that reflects how scripture itself frames them.

These gifts differ from the seventeen core gifts in three ways. First, their manifestation is typically private rather than public -- Paul's instruction in 1 Corinthians 14 is that the speaker in tongues address God personally if no interpreter is present, and the gift's primary location is personal prayer rather than corporate witness. This makes the trusted-person confirmation methodology unsuitable: the people closest to you cannot reliably observe a gift whose primary exercise is unseen. Second, the inclination questions used for the core gifts do not translate; what scripture asks is whether the believer has received the gift and whether its exercise has produced fruit, not whether they sense general inclination toward it. Third, the theological care required is unusually high. These gifts have produced both genuine blessing and significant abuse in the modern church, and the instrument addresses them with pastoral honesty rather than enthusiastic affirmation or dismissive skepticism.

The format for each charismatic gift entry includes the same essence statement, working definition, definition anchor, witnesses, and stewardship charge as the core gifts. Instead of three inclination questions and a community-confirmation question, it asks one direct-experience question and one fruitfulness question. The body-application and formation sections follow the core pattern, with adjustments for the gift's particular shape.

A user's results for these gifts are presented separately from the seventeen core gift results, with framing copy that explains the different methodology.

## 7.18 -- TONGUES

### CHARISMATIC GIFT -- VARIOUS KINDS OF TONGUES

# Tongues

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given ability to speak in a language not learned, for prayer, worship, or proclamation.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given ability to speak in a language the believer has not learned -- whether a known human language (as at Pentecost) or what Paul calls "tongues of angels" (1 Corinthians 13:1). The gift's primary location in the New Testament is private prayer and worship, where the believer's spirit prays beyond the limits of their understanding (1 Corinthians 14:2, 14). Its public exercise in the gathered church is permitted but tightly regulated by Paul: only when an interpreter is present, only one or two at most, in turn, for the building up of the body (1 Corinthians 14:26-28).

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is one of the most contested in the contemporary church, with three competing positions: cessationist (the gift has ceased entirely), Pentecostal (the gift is normative for Spirit-baptized believers and is the initial evidence of that baptism), and continuationist (the gift is real and active but not required of every believer, given according to the Spirit's distribution). The instrument takes the continuationist position. Tongues is real. It is given to some, not all. Paul explicitly says so in 1 Corinthians 12:30. The diagnostic question is whether the believer has personally received and exercised the gift in a way that has produced fruit -- intimate prayer, worship, spiritual edification -- consistent with scripture's description.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another various kinds of tongues..."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:10

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"And they were all filled with the Holy Spirit and began to speak in other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance... And at this sound the multitude came together, and they were bewildered, because each one was hearing them speak in his own language."*
> -- Acts 2:4, 6

*Pentecost -- the gift's inaugural manifestation. The tongues spoken were real human languages, recognized by the gathered crowd of pilgrims from across the empire. The gift in this form served as a sign of the Spirit's outpouring and an instrument for the gospel's proclamation across linguistic boundaries.*

> *"For one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to men but to God; for no one understands him, but he utters mysteries in the Spirit."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:2

*Paul's working definition of the gift's primary use. The speaker addresses God, not men. The content is "mysteries in the Spirit" -- prayer and worship that exceeds the believer's natural understanding. This is the gift's normal location: personal prayer life, not corporate spectacle.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"If any speak in a tongue, let there be only two or at most three, and each in turn, and let someone interpret. But if there is no one to interpret, let each of them keep silent in church and speak to himself and to God."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:27-28

*Paul's regulation is direct and binding. The gift's public exercise is permitted but bounded: two or three at most, in turn, with interpretation. Without an interpreter, the speaker is to remain silent in the corporate setting and exercise the gift privately, in personal prayer to God. The contemporary church has too often ignored these regulations; the instrument honors them.*

### Direct-Experience Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

Have you personally received and exercised the gift of tongues -- speaking in prayer or worship in a language you did not learn, as described in 1 Corinthians 14, in ways that have become a regular and meaningful part of your life with God?

### Fruitfulness Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

When you have exercised this gift in personal prayer or worship, how often has it produced visible fruit consistent with what scripture describes -- edification, deeper communion with God, the sense of praying beyond what you could express in your native language -- rather than becoming routine or performative?

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
The gift of tongues functions primarily in personal prayer -- the believer praying in tongues during personal devotion, intercession, or spiritual battle. It also operates in corporate worship contexts that honor Paul's regulations: with interpretation, in order, for the building up of the body. In some contexts, the gift surfaces in private prayer language during corporate worship without disruption to the gathering -- the believer praying quietly in tongues alongside the corporate service. The gift's most controversial public manifestation is the Pentecostal model of declared tongues with interpretation, which scripture permits but bounds by careful regulation.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer whose private prayer life is enriched by the gift -- prayer that exceeds the limits of their understanding, intercession that addresses what they could not articulate in their native language, worship that flows from depths the conscious mind cannot reach. A tongues-gifted believer functioning well does not require the gift to be exercised publicly to know its value, does not measure their spiritual life by whether they speak in tongues, and does not promote the gift as required of all believers. The gift, faithfully exercised, produces deeper communion with God without becoming an identity marker that divides the body.

**Common distortions.**
This gift has been the source of significant abuse in the modern church. It can become the marker of a spiritual elite -- the teaching that those who do not speak in tongues are second-class believers or have not received the Spirit. It can become performative: the public exercise of tongues without interpretation, in violation of Paul's clear regulation in 1 Corinthians 14. It can become formulaic: techniques for "receiving" tongues that pressure believers into producing speech they have not actually been given. The corrective is Paul's own framework: the gift is given to some, not all (1 Corinthians 12:30); its primary location is private prayer (1 Corinthians 14:2); its public exercise is bounded by the requirement of interpretation (1 Corinthians 14:27-28).

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Tongues pairs naturally with **Interpretation of Tongues** -- the partner gift that makes corporate exercise of tongues edifying for the gathered body. The two are designed by Paul to function together; tongues without interpretation in public is what he specifically prohibits. The gift also pairs with **Faith** (the conviction that exercises the gift confidently in personal prayer) and with **Prophecy** (where the believer who prays in tongues also receives direct prophetic words for the body).

### Formation Output for Active Tongues

*Displayed when responses indicate the gift is regularly exercised with fruit.*

The Spirit's work in you through the gift of tongues is to enable prayer and worship that exceeds the limits of your understanding -- communion with God that addresses what you could not articulate in your native language. This is what Paul describes in 1 Corinthians 14:2: not to men but to God, mysteries in the Spirit. The gift's primary location is your private prayer life. The fruit is deeper intimacy with God, intercession that exceeds your conscious capacity, and worship that flows from depths the surface cannot reach.

The stewardship of this gift requires careful honoring of Paul's regulations and resistance to the contemporary distortions. Exercise the gift faithfully in private; honor Paul's regulations in public; refuse the teaching that makes tongues a marker of spiritual class. The gift is yours, but it is not required of all believers, and the unity of the body is more important than the visibility of any one gift. Practice the gift in personal prayer. Let it deepen your communion with God. Resist any context that pressures you to perform it or that frames it as more important than the fruit of the Spirit. And remember that Paul prefers prophecy to tongues for the building up of the church (1 Corinthians 14:5) -- the gift is for your edification first, and for the body's only under the conditions Paul specifies.

#### Anchor Scripture

> *"For one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to men but to God; for no one understands him, but he utters mysteries in the Spirit."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:2

#### Weekly Practice

This week, spend deliberate, unhurried time in personal prayer using the gift of tongues. Pray for specific people, situations, and burdens -- not for the gift's own sake, but for the intercession the gift enables. Notice what the Spirit does in you and through your prayer. The gift's fruit is deeper communion with God and intercession that exceeds your natural capacity; the practice that sustains the gift is sustained, private prayer.

#### Rule of Life Crosslink
This gift connects to the **Prayer rhythm** and the **Solitude rhythm** in your Rule of Life. The gift's primary location is private prayer; without sustained solitude and prayer, the gift has no soil in which to function meaningfully.

### Edge Case Outputs

#### Emerging or Uncertain Tongues
*Displayed when the user has had some experience of the gift but has not exercised it regularly with sustained fruit.*

Your responses suggest you may have had experience of this gift but have not yet exercised it as a regular, fruitful part of your life with God. This is common. The gift, when present, often develops over time through faithful practice. Find a mature, accountable believer in your community who exercises this gift faithfully and submit your sense of it to them. Practice the gift in private prayer. Test the fruit over time -- whether your communion with God deepens, whether your intercession becomes more sustained, whether the gift produces the edification scripture describes.

#### Tongues Not Present
*Displayed when the user has not received the gift.*

This gift is not where the Spirit has chosen to work in your life. Paul's question in 1 Corinthians 12:30 is rhetorical with an implied negative answer: do all speak with tongues? No, they do not. The gift is given to some, not all, according to the Spirit's distribution. The absence of this gift is no commentary on your spiritual life, your maturity, or your reception of the Spirit. Every believer who has trusted Christ has received the Holy Spirit; not every believer has received this particular gift. Honor the believers in your community who exercise it faithfully under Paul's regulations, and look to the gifts where the Spirit is more clearly at work in you. The Spirit's distribution is sovereign and can shift -- some believers report receiving this gift in later seasons of their walk. The picture is of this season, not a permanent verdict.

## 7.19 -- INTERPRETATION OF TONGUES

### CHARISMATIC GIFT -- INTERPRETATION

# Interpretation of Tongues

### Essence Statement
*The Spirit-given ability to make a tongue intelligible to the gathered body for its edification.*

### Working Definition
The Spirit-given ability to render a spoken tongue into the language of the gathered church so that what was said becomes intelligible and edifying for the body. Interpretation is not literal translation; Paul's word implies the conveyance of the meaning, sense, and direction of the tongue rather than a word-for-word rendering. The gift exists specifically to make the corporate exercise of tongues fruitful for the church -- without interpretation, Paul explicitly forbids public tongues (1 Corinthians 14:28). The gift is the necessary complement to tongues in the gathered setting.

### Diagnostic Challenge
This gift is even less commonly addressed than tongues in contemporary church culture. It is easily confused with general spiritual sensitivity, the impression that one knows what a public tongue meant, or the prepared interpretation offered without genuine Spirit-given empowerment. The signature of the gift is the consistent ability, when a public tongue is spoken, to render its meaning in the language of the body in ways that produce edification, encouragement, or instruction -- and that subsequent testing of the interpretation confirms it as from the Spirit.

### Definition Anchor

> *"...to another the interpretation of tongues."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 12:10

### Manifestation Witnesses

> *"If any speak in a tongue, let there be only two or at most three, and each in turn, and let someone interpret."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:27

*Paul's regulation for corporate tongues necessarily implies the gift of interpretation. The instruction "let someone interpret" assumes that the Spirit has gifted at least one believer in the gathering with the corresponding ability. Without this gift in operation, the gift of tongues cannot be exercised publicly according to scripture.*

> *"Therefore, one who speaks in a tongue should pray that he may interpret."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:13

*Paul's instruction that the tongues-speaker should also pray to interpret. The two gifts may operate in the same believer, and Paul's framing suggests that the integrated exercise is the most edifying form. The gift of interpretation is sometimes given to the same believer who speaks in tongues, and sometimes to a different believer in the gathered body.*

### Stewardship Charge

> *"Let all things be done for building up."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:26

*Paul's controlling principle for the use of all charismatic gifts in the gathered church. Interpretation, in particular, exists to make tongues edifying. The gift's purpose is the body's building up. Interpretation that does not produce edification has either misread the tongue or has drifted from the gift's scriptural function.*

### Direct-Experience Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

When a public tongue has been spoken in a gathered context, have you received and offered an interpretation -- a Spirit-given understanding of the tongue's meaning, rendered in the language of the body -- that the gathered church received as edifying and that has been confirmed by other gifted believers as consistent with the Spirit's work?

### Fruitfulness Question

*5-point frequency scale: Never / Rarely / Sometimes / Often / Consistently*

When you have offered an interpretation, how often has the gathered body been visibly edified, encouraged, or instructed by what you shared -- and how often has subsequent testing by mature believers confirmed that the interpretation was from the Spirit rather than from your own thinking?

### How This Gift Serves the Body

**Where it shows up.**
The gift of interpretation functions almost exclusively in corporate worship contexts where the gift of tongues is also being exercised publicly -- typically in churches and gatherings that honor Paul's regulations and practice the charismatic gifts in order. In churches that do not practice the corporate exercise of tongues, this gift has fewer occasions to manifest, though some believers who carry it report receiving impressions of meaning when they hear tongues spoken in personal prayer contexts or recorded ministries. The gift is uncommon, even more so than tongues itself, and its operation requires the corresponding presence of public tongues to be exercised meaningfully.

**What it looks like functioning well.**
The believer who consistently receives and offers interpretations that the gathered body receives as edifying. The interpreter whose work over time has been confirmed by mature, accountable leadership in their church as Spirit-given rather than constructed. An interpretation-gifted believer functioning well operates under the same regulations Paul gives for prophecy -- the interpretation is submitted, tested, and weighed by the elders or pastoral leadership of the church (1 Corinthians 14:29). They are quick to say "I think the Spirit gave me this" rather than "thus saith the Lord," and they welcome correction.

**Common distortions.**
This gift, when distorted, can produce some of the most damaging dynamics in charismatic worship. It can become prepared performance: the interpreter who offers the same generic "interpretation" regardless of what was actually spoken in tongues. It can become competitive: multiple believers offering competing interpretations without submission to testing. It can become a tool of control: the leader who interprets others' tongues in ways that direct the congregation, exact compliance, or assert authority. The corrective is Paul's framework throughout 1 Corinthians 14: order, accountability, testing, and the orienting purpose of the body's edification.

**Gifts it pairs with.**
Interpretation pairs primarily with **Tongues** -- the gift it complements and makes corporately useful. It also pairs with **Prophecy** (the gift it most closely resembles in function, since the rendered interpretation often takes the form of a word for the body) and with **Discernment of Spirits** (which protects the interpreter from offering interpretations that are not from God).

### Formation Output for Active Interpretation

*Displayed when responses indicate the gift is regularly exercised with fruit.*

The Spirit's work in you through the gift of interpretation is to make the gift of tongues edifying for the gathered body. This is what Paul describes in 1 Corinthians 14:13, 26-28: the interpreter renders the meaning of the tongue so that the church may be built up. The gift is uncommon and is exercised under regulation. It is also vital -- without it in operation, the gift of tongues cannot be exercised publicly according to scripture.

The stewardship of this gift requires the same accountability framework that governs prophecy. Submit your interpretations to the testing of mature leadership in your church. Refuse the temptation to offer prepared or generic interpretations. Welcome correction. Resist any context that pressures you to interpret without genuine Spirit-given impression, or that uses interpretation as a tool of control. The gift's purpose is the body's edification, and its faithful exercise requires the humility to be wrong, to be tested, and to be silent when the Spirit has not given you a word.

#### Anchor Scripture

> *"Let all things be done for building up."*
> -- 1 Corinthians 14:26

#### Weekly Practice

If your church practices the corporate exercise of tongues and you have been called to interpret, continue in faithful submission to your pastor and elders. When an interpretation comes to you, write it down before sharing it where possible. Submit it for testing. Welcome correction. If your church does not practice corporate tongues, exercise the gift in the contexts where it has been confirmed and remain attentive to the Spirit's leading without seeking opportunities the Spirit has not opened.

#### Rule of Life Crosslink
This gift connects to the **Prayer rhythm** and the **Community rhythm** in your Rule of Life. Prayer provides the listening through which the interpretation comes; community provides the accountability that protects the gift from drifting into the believer's own thinking.

### Edge Case Outputs

#### Emerging or Uncertain Interpretation
*Displayed when the user has had some experience of the gift but has not exercised it regularly with sustained, confirmed fruit.*

Your responses suggest you may have received impressions of meaning when tongues have been spoken, but the gift has not yet been confirmed through consistent, edifying exercise under accountable leadership. Submit any impressions to your pastor or to mature, accountable believers before sharing them publicly. Practice the discipline of writing down what you sense, testing it carefully, and only offering it when it holds up under scrutiny. The gift, if it is real, will be confirmed over time through the body's edification.

#### Interpretation Not Present
*Displayed when the user has not received the gift.*

This gift is not where the Spirit has chosen to work in your life. The gift of interpretation is uncommon, and its absence in a believer's life is no commentary on their faithfulness or maturity. Honor the interpretation-gifted believers in your community when they emerge, and look to the gifts where the Spirit is more clearly at work in you. The Spirit's gifts are distributed as he wills, and that distribution can shift -- the picture is of this season, not a permanent verdict.

## 10. SCORING LOGIC

This section specifies the scoring computation in technical detail sufficient for implementation. The architecture is straightforward but must be implemented precisely; the methodology is the instrument's primary claim to integrity, and scoring errors would undermine the entire purpose.

### 10.1 Score Components Per Gift

For each of the 17 core gifts, three sub-scores are computed:

**Inclination Score (0-100):**
- Average of the user's three inclination question responses, mapped from the five-point scale:
  - Strongly disagree = 0
  - Disagree = 25
  - Neither = 50
  - Agree = 75
  - Strongly agree = 100
- Skipped responses (the "skip this question" option) are recorded as 50 (neutral) but flagged internally; if more than one of the three inclination questions for a single gift is skipped, the gift's inclination score is marked as low-confidence
- Final inclination score = mean of the three responses

**Fruitfulness Score (0-100):**
- The user's single fruitfulness question response, mapped from the five-point frequency scale:
  - Never = 0
  - Rarely = 25
  - Sometimes = 50
  - Often = 75
  - Consistently = 100
- Skipped fruitfulness responses are recorded as 50 (neutral) but flagged internally; a skipped fruitfulness response marks the gift's fruitfulness score as low-confidence

**Confirmation Score (0-100 or null):**
- For each trusted person who responded with anything other than "I haven't been in a position to see this":
  - Their response is mapped from the five-point frequency scale (same mapping as fruitfulness)
- Confirmation score = mean of all non-null trusted-person responses for that gift
- If all trusted persons selected "I haven't been in a position to see this" for a particular gift (or no trusted persons have responded yet), the confirmation score is null and the composite is computed using only inclination and fruitfulness (see 10.2)

### 10.2 Composite Score Per Gift

**With confirmation data present (any trusted-person responses for this gift):**
- Composite = (Inclination × 0.30) + (Fruitfulness × 0.30) + (Confirmation × 0.40)

**Without confirmation data (no trusted-person responses or all "haven't been in a position to see this" for this gift):**
- Composite = (Inclination × 0.50) + (Fruitfulness × 0.50)
- The gift is flagged as "pending confirmation" -- the composite is computed but the results display indicates that the full picture awaits trusted-person input

### 10.3 Tier Classification

Gifts are classified into three tiers based on composite score and confirmation status:

**Active:** Composite ≥ 70 AND at least two trusted persons have provided non-null responses for this gift
- Displayed with full Formation Output for Active

**Active Pending Confirmation:** Composite ≥ 70 AND fewer than two trusted persons have provided non-null responses for this gift
- Displayed with Active formation output but with a "pending confirmation" indicator and a note that the classification may shift when trusted-person responses are in

**Emerging:** Composite 50-69, regardless of confirmation status
- Displayed with Emerging edge-case copy

**Quiet:** Composite below 50, regardless of confirmation status
- Displayed in the de-emphasized Quiet section with Quiet edge-case copy

### 10.4 Gap Detection

After all trusted-person responses are in, the system checks for two specific gap patterns and surfaces them in the "Worth Paying Attention To" section of the results:

**Inclination-Confirmation Gap:**
- User's inclination score is ≥ 70 for a gift
- Confirmation score for that gift is ≤ 40
- Displayed with the Inclination-Confirmation Gap edge-case copy from that gift's entry

**Confirmation-Inclination Gap:**
- Confirmation score for a gift is ≥ 70
- User's inclination score for that gift is ≤ 50
- Displayed with the Confirmation-Inclination Gap edge-case copy from that gift's entry

Gaps are only surfaced when at least two trusted persons have provided non-null responses for the gift in question. Single-source confirmation is not enough to claim a gap.

### 10.5 Charismatic Gifts Scoring

The two charismatic gifts use a simplified scoring framework:

**Active charismatic gift:** Direct-experience response = "Often" or "Consistently" AND Fruitfulness response = "Often" or "Consistently"
- Displayed with Formation Output for Active

**Emerging or Uncertain charismatic gift:** Direct-experience response = "Sometimes" OR (any response combination not meeting Active criteria but not entirely Never/Rarely)
- Displayed with Emerging or Uncertain edge-case copy

**Not present:** Direct-experience response = "Never" or "Rarely"
- Displayed with "Not Present" edge-case copy

### 10.6 Edge Cases in Scoring

**No trusted-person responses received:**
- User's results display all gifts as "Pending Confirmation"
- The results are valid as a draft but the methodology section explicitly notes that confirmation is the heaviest-weighted stream and the picture is incomplete

**Partial trusted-person responses:**
- Some trusted persons responded, others did not
- The confirmation score is computed from those who did respond, weighted equally
- After three trusted persons have responded, additional responses are still included in the average but the methodology is considered complete

**Inconsistent trusted-person responses:**
- High variance among trusted-person responses for a single gift is flagged internally but not displayed to the user; the average is used
- Internal flagging allows for future refinement of the methodology

**Low-confidence flags:**
- Any gift with low-confidence inclination, fruitfulness, or confirmation (multiple skips or missing data) is flagged
- Low-confidence gifts are still scored and classified but display a small "low-confidence" indicator in the results, with a tooltip explaining that the user can retake portions of the assessment to improve accuracy

## 12. COPY RULES

This section specifies the voice and tone standards that govern every word of copy in the assessment, the Constellation, the results, and the trusted-person flow. The standards are an instance of the broader CF Voice and Style Guide and must be applied without exception.

### 12.1 Tone Standards

- First person throughout for the user-facing copy; second person for direct address
- Earnest, convicted, warm -- never sarcastic, detached, or performatively enthusiastic
- Christian language used naturally and unapologetically; the audience is believers; no need to soften theological language
- Avoid both the platform-charismatic register ("God told me to tell you...") and the dry academic register ("This study examines...")
- The instrument is pastoral, not technical -- even the methodology sections speak to the user as a believer being introduced to a tool, not as a researcher reading documentation
- Avoid em dashes in copy -- use double hyphens ( -- ) for any pause or aside that requires that punctuation in casual contexts, or restructure to use commas, colons, or a new sentence
- Oxford comma always
- Semicolons permitted in formal sections (methodology, theological framing) to connect related clauses
- Numbers written out in formal copy (seventeen core gifts), numerals in technical sections, headers, and bullet lists

### 12.2 Banned Words and Phrases

Apply the CF style guide's forbidden language to all copy. The most relevant prohibitions for this assessment:

- No "leverage," "utilize," "harness," "unlock," "transformative," "groundbreaking"
- No "journey" used metaphorically for a process or progression
- No "tapestry," "landscape," "ecosystem," "paradigm" used as metaphors
- No "It's not X, it's Y" sentence pattern
- No self-posed rhetorical questions answered immediately ("The best part? Y.")
- No "at the end of the day," "in conclusion," "let's dive in," "let's unpack"
- No "fast-paced world," "now more than ever," "in today's digital age"

### 12.3 Required Phrases and Signature Language

Preserve and use where contextually appropriate:
- "pockets of unknown wounding" (CF signature phrase)
- "go back to go forward" (CF signature phrase)
- "release your grip" (CF signature phrase)

Use language that consistently reinforces the theological framing:
- "Where the Spirit is at work through you" (preferred over "your spiritual gifts")
- "The Spirit's particular empowerment" (preferred over "your ability")
- "Evidence of what God is doing" (preferred over "your gifts are...")
- "Confirmed in the body" (preferred over "validated by others")
- "Tested in service" (preferred over "applied" or "exercised")

### 12.4 Headers and Labels

- Page titles and section subheads use Cormorant Garamond, sentence case
- Eyebrow labels use Michroma small caps, uppercase
- Body copy uses Inter for UI elements, Cormorant Garamond for prose, both at generous line heights
- Buttons use Michroma small caps, uppercase, with a slight letter-spacing increase

### 12.5 Scripture References

- All scripture quoted is ESV
- Scripture appears in blockquotes within prose sections
- Inline scripture references (e.g., "1 Corinthians 12:7-11") use the existing CF `ScriptureRef` component for hover-to-preview functionality
- Where multiple translations would meaningfully clarify a passage, use the ESV in the main copy and note alternatives in editorial brackets if necessary (rare)
- Scripture is never paraphrased without indication; if the source is paraphrased, the reference includes "paraphrase" notation

### 13.3 Pairing Symmetry Validation

The validator in `src/data/gifts/index.js` runs at import time and enforces the data contract for all 19 gifts:
- Exactly 19 gifts must be present, with unique keys and valid categories (manifestation, ministry, equipping, charismatic)
- All required fields per gift must be present (workingDefinition, scriptureAnchor, manifestationWitnesses, stewardshipCharge, fruitfulnessQuestion, bodyApplication, pairsWith, formationOutput, edgeCases)
- Core gifts must have exactly 3 `inclinationQuestions`, a `communityConfirmationQuestion`, and the four core edge cases (emerging, inclinationConfirmationGap, confirmationInclinationGap, quiet)
- Charismatic gifts must have a `directExperienceQuestion` (no `inclinationQuestions` or `communityConfirmationQuestion`) and the two charismatic edge cases (emerging, notPresent)
- `pairsWith` symmetry: if gift A's `pairsWith` includes gift B's key, gift B's `pairsWith` must include gift A's key
- The validator throws a single aggregated error on failure, surfacing all issues at once and failing the app at boot rather than silently rendering broken constellation lines

### 13.4 State Persistence

- Assessment progress is saved to localStorage on every question response
- Users with accounts have results persisted to the backend
- Trusted-person responses are persisted server-side via the token-based route, with the token tying the response to the specific user-trusted-person pairing
- A user's "Formation Picture" view pulls from both the Fruit and Gifts results stored against their account

### 13.5 The Integrated Reflection Generator

The custom reflection paragraph in the "Formation Picture" view (Section 11.1, Section 3: The Integration) is generated by a small Claude API call:

- Input to the API: user's top fruit, user's top active gift, brief excerpts from the relevant fruit and gift entries
- Output: a 100-150 word reflection on how these two might function together in the user's life
- The prompt includes explicit instructions to follow CF voice and style standards (no em dashes, Oxford comma, no banned words, etc.)
- The reflection is cached on first generation and only regenerates if the user retakes either assessment

### 13.6 Mobile Performance

- The Constellation visualization on mobile uses simplified rendering (no connection-line animations, reduced glow effects) to maintain performance on lower-end devices
- The assessment itself is lightweight and performant on mobile; the one-question-per-screen architecture supports this naturally
- Images and animations use `loading="lazy"` and `will-change` hints appropriately
- The trusted-person assessment, in particular, must load quickly on mobile, since trusted persons will often complete it on their phones

- Results URLs are private by default; sharing requires an explicit user action that generates a shareable link with a separate token

## 15. POST-RESULTS CTA FLOW

This section specifies where users go after they have seen their results, completed the trusted-person flow, and explored the integrated Formation Picture.

### 15.1 First-Visit Results

When a user first sees their results (before trusted-person responses are in):
- Primary CTA: `SEND TRUSTED-PERSON INVITATIONS →` (already specified in Section 8.5)
- Secondary CTA: `Take the Fruit of the Spirit Assessment →` (if not already completed)
- Tertiary: small links to `Read more about each gift`, `Share your results`, `Save as PDF`

### 15.2 After Trusted-Person Responses Are In

When the user receives notification that responses have come in:
- Email notification (or in-app notification) with copy: *Two of the people you invited have weighed in. Your formation picture has been updated.* CTA: `SEE YOUR UPDATED RESULTS →`
- On returning to results, a small banner at the top: *Updated with input from [N] trusted person[s]. The full picture is now in view.*
- The "Worth Paying Attention To" section (gap detection) appears if applicable

### 15.3 After Both Assessments Are Complete

The user is directed to the integrated Formation Picture view, which becomes the primary destination:
- Email/in-app notification: *Your Formation Picture is ready -- both assessments complete.* CTA: `SEE YOUR FORMATION PICTURE →`
- The Formation Picture view becomes the new "home" of the user's formation profile within the Field Guide

### 15.4 Ongoing Formation Pathway (v2 Roadmap)

For v1 launch, the Formation Picture view ends with a CTA to the CF newsletter and a note that formation pathways are coming. For v2 (post-launch roadmap), the Formation Picture will hand off to:
- Curated formation content specific to the user's gifts and fruit
- Suggested next steps for spiritual practice
- Community discussions among believers with similar formation profiles
- A regular check-in cadence (quarterly retakes, with longitudinal tracking)

These are flagged for the roadmap but not implemented in v1.
